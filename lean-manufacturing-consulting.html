Lean Manufacturing Consulting

Improve production flow and reduce everyday waste.

Leanova helps manufacturing teams examine how materials, information and work move through production. The aim is to remove activities that do not add customer value while making the process easier to manage.

Discuss this business need →All service areas →

Where it helps

Long queues between operations, excess work-in-progress, repeated searching, inconsistent work methods and frequent changeovers can hide capacity and delay orders. Start with a specific product family or production area rather than changing everything at once.

How we approach the work

Understand customer requirements and map the current value stream. Observe constraints with the team, agree a target condition and prioritize practical changes. Relevant methods already in Leanova’s service scope include 5S, Kaizen, standardized work, visual management, Kanban, line balancing and SMED.

What to measure

Agree a baseline for lead time, work-in-progress, changeover time, first-pass quality or on-time delivery. Review the measures after changes and check whether improvement at one station helps the whole flow. Targets depend on the starting conditions and agreed scope.

Illustrative application

A production team with queues before a bottleneck could map the flow, examine changeovers and rebalance work. The team would compare throughput, queue size and delivery reliability before extending the changes. This is an example of an approach, not a reported client result.

Guided by ASF™

The Adaptive Systems Framework connects the business context, constraints and improvement priorities. Choose methods to fit the problem, then measure outcomes and adapt.

Who it is for

Plant heads, production managers and owners of manufacturing units where output, lead time or inventory are hurting delivery.

Typical deliverables

  • Current-state value stream map with waiting time, inventory and constraints marked
  • Prioritised improvement plan with owners and measures
  • Standard work, visual management and 5S standards for pilot areas
  • Kaizen events run with your team, plus a follow-up review of results

Final deliverables are agreed for each engagement.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a Lean engagement take?

It depends on scope. A diagnostic usually comes first. Improvement is then planned in phases, so you can see results in a pilot area before extending it.

Do we need to stop production?

No. Observation, mapping and most kaizen work happen alongside normal production. Changeover or layout trials are scheduled with you.

Do you share results from other clients?

Only when a client has approved publication and the results are verified. Until then, we explain our approach and show labelled illustrative examples.

What happens after I send an enquiry?

We reply by email to understand the process, the problem and your constraints, then suggest a sensible first step. That might be a short diagnostic visit or a call.
